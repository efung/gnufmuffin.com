It's been a tough month, but I did find some time to make a challah for
a meal we were hosting this Friday night. The addition of turmeric
is what is supposed to make it golden, but this bread was a letdown.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/55512345118/in/photostream/" title="Golden Challah"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55512345118_9f102d82b6_c.jpg" width="800" height="600" alt="Golden Challah"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

The [recipe](https://aish.com/golden-challah/) is by
Adeena Sussman, a food writer and author who wrote a cookbook we like,
_Sababa_. I was very intrigued by the addition of this vibrant spice,
but it didn't really make much of a difference that I could see,
which was disappointing.  It certainly didn't taste of turmeric, though,
but that is actually a good thing.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/55512345118/in/photostream/" title="Golden Challah"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55511227597_924fefc678_c.jpg" width="600" height="800" alt="Golden Challah"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

This is a perfectly fine challah, but I guess I was expecting some
kind of alchemy from turmeric. To get a nice golden loaf consistently, I've 
found using really orange egg yolks to be reliable.
