Regular readers will know that we have two plum trees in our front yard. This
was the first year in recent memory that we got a great harvest from the smaller,
Japanese-type plum tree. With such a great haul, we needed to use them up quickly.

What we ended up doing was making plum sauce! My favourite dip with fried foods, 
like spring rolls, tempura shrimp, chicken fingers, etc. is a papaya mustard
from a Jean-Georges Vongerichten cookbook. But I also have a fun preserving book
called _Batch_ which featured this savory sauce.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/55512405453/in/photostream/" title="Sweet and Sour Plum Sauce"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55512405453_d7c51a650d_c.jpg" width="800" height="600" alt="Sweet and Sour Plum Sauce"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

Starting with a puree of pitted plums (skins on!), you add in lots of assertive
ingredients: cider vinegar, honey, rum, soy, garlic, ginger. Then dashes of
spices like cinnamon, cayenne and cloves. 

It's interesting how two cooks can "follow" the same recipe and end
up with two different tasting end results: Dr. S' version was more tart
as she used cider vinegar as written. In my batch, I skipped the vinegar
(I worried that the plums were too tart already) but mine used an older
knob of ginger, so its flavour came out more.
