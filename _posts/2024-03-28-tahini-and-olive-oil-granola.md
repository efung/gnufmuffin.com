Dr. S purchased a cookbook recently, called Sababa by Adeena Sussman.
It had an interesting take on granola that I really wanted to try. And
it was definitely worth the effort.

The [recipe](https://cookbooksonrepeat.com/tahini-and-olive-oil-granola/) 
calls for the addition of a generous amount of tahini paste, as well
as date syrup and olive oil. The mixture was quite 
[squidgy](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/squidgy)
and I thought I had made a mistake.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/53666291181/in/dateposted/" title="Tahini Granola"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53666291181_92cece1c34_c.jpg" width="600" height="800" alt="Tahini Granola"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

But towards the end of the baking time, when everything is a deep
golden brown, it starts to dry out. And then, after cooling, the clusters
magically crisp up, ready to be mixed with dried fruit.

I really liked the slightly bitter flavor of this granola, and there's
no mistaking that it's from the tahini. Highly recommended.
