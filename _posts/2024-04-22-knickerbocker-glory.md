For Passover this year, we were at my brother-in-law's house, and I 
planned on making an exciting, frozen treat from the _Sweet_ cookbook.
We've made ice creams and sorbets in the past for this Jewish holiday,
but this is the first time we've done assemble-it-yourself sundaes.

You can read more about [knickerbocker glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knickerbocker_glory) on Wikipedia. There are many versions out there
but it seems that it's most popular in England. What caught my eye
about this [recipe](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2017/sep/02/pavlova-cheesecake-yotam-ottolenghi-dessert-millionaires-shortbread-knickerbocker-glory-recipes-sweet#knickerbocker-glory)
is that the ice cream is actually a semifreddo, which doesn't need 
churning. Perfect to make at someone else's house.

The most time-consuming part was just sieving the pureed, frozen 
raspberries. Once that's done, heating the custard base over a bain
marie, whipping it, etc. is pretty straightforward. We took a shortcut
and used some candied pecans from a store.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/53748659591/in/photostream/" title="Knickerbocker Glory"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53748659591_d2b110d8e1_c.jpg" width="800" height="600" alt="Knickerbocker Glory"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

After dinner, we laid everything out and allowed people to assemble
their own dessert. The raspberry coulis turned out to be the most
popular item. Everyone loved this dessert, with its temperature 
and sweet-tart contrasts, and I especially liked how every spoonful 
was a little bit different.

We made this again for Gabe's birthday a month later to rave reviews
again. Highly recommended.
