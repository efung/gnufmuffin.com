With the last leftover pie dough round, I finally got a chance to right some wrongs.
After the pair of pecan-less pecan pies I made earlier this week, I'm pleased
to report that we got to enjoy this chocolate variation for dessert on Friday night.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/55607981242/in/dateposted/" title="Chocolate Pecan Pie"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55607981242_1b5a9eaf03_c.jpg" width="600" height="800" alt="Chocolate Pecan Pie"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

The [recipe](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11942-chocolate-pecan-pie)
was originally published in Cook's Country, Oct/Nov 2019 but the online version was updated
tp be "more foolproof" in a Nov 2020 revision. The filling calls for just 2 ounces of 
unsweetened chocolate, which I substituted with 70% bittersweet and a few tablespoons less 
brown sugar.

<a data-flickr-embed="true" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/gnuf/55609129033/in/photostream/" title="Chocolate Pecan Pie"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55609129033_a7c228b8cc_c.jpg" width="800" height="600" alt="Chocolate Pecan Pie"/></a><script async src="//embedr.flickr.com/assets/client-code.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

That small addition of chocolate turns this classic Thanksgiving
dessert into something more sophisticated and memorable. I wouldn't
say it's better than a regular pecan pie, but it's a worthy variation
to have in your repertoire. The chocolate flavour is not overpowering,
but balanced.
